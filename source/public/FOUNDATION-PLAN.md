**I’d give you three challenging lifting days and two light support days.** With one month of lifting experience and four hours of intense Saturday badminton, five hard gym sessions would conflict with your recovery requirement. Saturday is a rest day **from weights only**; Wednesday is your only full rest day.

This six-week foundation block keeps your requested five gym days and exactly eight exercises per session. It develops strength, muscle, landing control, and badminton power while gradually increasing your workload.

| Day | Training emphasis | Approximate duration |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Lower-body strength, unilateral work, vertical power | 60–75 min |
| Tuesday | Upper-body strength, rotational power, shoulder conditioning | 65–80 min |
| Wednesday | Full rest | — |
| Thursday | Full body, lateral movement, posterior chain | 65–80 min |
| Friday | Light shoulder, arm, foot and core support | 25–35 min |

The rotation is **easy Sunday → lower Monday → upper Tuesday**, then **full-body Thursday → easy Friday → hard court Saturday → easy Sunday**. Tuesday uses supported upper-body exercises to limit additional leg and lower-back fatigue.

Read these rules before starting:

- **RPE 6 / 7 / 8:** finish with approximately **4 / 3 / 2 good repetitions remaining**. RPE 3–5 means easy practice, well away from failure.
- **Weeks 1–2:** cap strength exercises at **two sets and RPE 6**. Keep any lower intensity prescribed below. Use the non-jumping versions described later.
- **Weeks 3–5:** use the tables if soreness and court performance remain manageable. **Week 6:** halve working sets, rounding up, and cap strength work at RPE 6.
- Sets are working sets; warm-ups are additional. Repetitions are **per side when stated**. For unilateral exercises, allow about 15–30 seconds to switch sides, then take the listed rest after both sides.
- Your **80 kg deadlift does not establish your 1RM or your Romanian-deadlift load**. Select weights using controlled technique and the prescribed RPE.

Training to failure is unnecessary for this block; current ACSM guidance supports progressive resistance training without requiring failure on each set. [ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/)

**How to progress the block**

1. **Introduce impact gradually.** During weeks 1–2, replace Monday’s jump with a quick squat-to-calf raise, without leaving the floor. Thursday stays a lateral step-and-stick. From week 3, introduce low bilateral jumps if walking, stairs and landing practice are comfortable. Introduce small lateral bounds only after bilateral landings are consistently controlled and symptom-free during and the following day. Keep the same exercise slot, sets and reps.
2. **Treat power repetitions separately from lifting RPE.** For throws and jumps, RPE describes overall effort—not repetitions remaining before failure. Reset fully; stop when speed, balance or landing quality deteriorates. Do not add weighted jumps or depth jumps during this block.
3. **Progress repetitions before weight.** When every set reaches the top of its range at the target RPE on two exposures, add the smallest available load. Have a gym coach check your squat, hinge and landing technique early in the block.
4. **Match unilateral work.** Start with the less coordinated side and match its clean repetitions on the other side. Do not automatically add extra sets to the nondominant side.
5. **Respond to court fatigue.** If Saturday leaves you moving differently or sore on stairs, keep Sunday comfortable and replace Monday’s jumps with the non-jumping version. Reduce Monday’s lower-body sets by roughly one-third. If Thursday repeatedly makes Saturday footwork sluggish, reduce Thursday’s lower-body exercises to one set until that resolves.
6. **Respond to pain.** Stop an exercise that causes sharp or increasing joint/tendon pain. Persistent pain, swelling or altered walking warrants assessment; completing eight exercises does not take priority over that.

At the full table dose, you have approximately **nine quadriceps sets, seven hamstring sets, six direct chest sets plus landmine pressing, and eight back sets weekly**. That is a reasonable starting amount for a novice pursuing muscle retention or growth alongside badminton. The light support sessions and power drills are not counted as hard hypertrophy sets.

Finally, **110 → 90 kg by December 31 requires losing 1.25 kg/week over the remaining 16 weeks**. I would initially aim nearer **0.5–0.8 kg/week**, adjusting food intake against your weight trend, strength and court performance. Gradual loss is generally more sustainable, and a small athlete trial favored slower loss for preserving or gaining lean mass during strength training. [CDC guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html), [athlete weight-loss trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21558571/)

Treat 90 kg as a destination whose deadline may need adjusting. Neither that weight nor this program establishes that you will be at 10–11% body fat.
